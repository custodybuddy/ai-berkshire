## 1. Executive Summary

Canadian railways are high-quality infrastructure businesses, but for a beginner with only CAD 1,000, **the biggest decision is not CN versus CPKC; it is whether to own one railway stock at all**.

**Base-case conclusion:** CN is the more conservative railway pick today because it has a broader Canadian network, stronger current dividend yield, lower PE, lower P/FCF, and higher current ROE. CPKC is the higher-growth story because it uniquely connects Canada, the United States, and Mexico after the Kansas City Southern combination, but the stock screens more expensive and has lower current free-cash-flow yield.

**Simple answer for a beginner:**

| Choice | Research view |
|---|---|
| Buy one railway stock | Prefer CN for conservative beginner exposure; prefer CPKC only if the investor specifically wants the North America trade-corridor growth story. |
| Split between CN and CPKC | Reasonable if the investor wants railway exposure but does not want to choose one operator. |
| Avoid single-stock concentration | Most prudent if CAD 1,000 is the investor's first or only investment allocation. Use a diversified Canadian or global ETF instead, then add railway exposure later. |

## 2. Railway Business in Plain Language

A railway company owns and operates tracks, locomotives, terminals, yards, and logistics systems. It moves heavy goods over long distances: grain, containers, cars, chemicals, energy products, lumber, metals, and consumer goods.

The business is attractive because:

- Rail is cheaper and more fuel-efficient than trucking for heavy long-haul freight.
- Rail networks are hard to replicate because rights-of-way, land, permits, and network density took more than a century to build.
- Customers often need rail access for bulk commodities and intermodal containers.
- Once the track network exists, efficiency improvements can create strong operating leverage.

The business is risky because:

- Rail volumes depend on the economy.
- The business is capital intensive.
- Weather, labour, derailments, regulation, and fuel costs matter.
- A railway can be a great business and still be a poor investment if bought at too high a valuation.

**One-sentence business definition:** A railway earns money by charging customers to move freight across an irreplaceable physical network.

## 7. Bull Case

### CN Bull Case

CN owns a hard-to-replicate network, generates strong margins, has a long dividend-growth record, and currently screens better on valuation. If freight volumes recover, operating efficiency improves, and management continues disciplined capital returns, CN can compound steadily.

### CPKC Bull Case

CPKC is the only single-line railway connecting Canada, the United States, and Mexico. If nearshoring, North American manufacturing, cross-border trade, and intermodal conversion from trucks to rail continue, CPKC could grow faster than CN. The KCS merger may still have years of synergy and route-density upside.

### Sector Bull Case

Railways benefit from:

- High barriers to entry.
- Better fuel efficiency than trucking.
- Long-lived physical infrastructure.
- Pricing power when networks are constrained.
- Potential intermodal growth as shippers seek cost and emissions advantages.

---

## 8. Bear Case and Key Risks

### Economic Slowdown

Railways are tied to industrial activity and consumer-goods movement. A recession can reduce carloads, intermodal volumes, autos, metals, forest products, and energy shipments.

### Fuel Costs

Fuel is a major expense. Railways often use fuel surcharges, but timing differences and customer contracts can still hurt margins.

### Regulation

Railways are essential infrastructure. Governments and regulators can intervene on safety, pricing, service obligations, labour, competition, and cross-border trade.

### Labour

Railways depend on skilled unionized labour. Strikes, labour shortages, scheduling disputes, or wage inflation can disrupt operations and raise costs.

### Capital Intensity

Rail networks require constant spending on track, bridges, locomotives, terminals, safety systems, and technology. Free cash flow is meaningful, but maintenance and growth capex are not optional.

### Weather and Safety

Extreme cold, floods, wildfires, derailments, and accidents can disrupt operations and create liabilities.

### Valuation Risk

Both stocks trade at premium valuations compared with ordinary cyclical businesses. If interest rates rise or growth disappoints, the market may apply lower multiples.

## 9. Beginner Single-Stock Concentration Question

CAD 1,000 in one railway stock is not reckless if it is a small part of a larger diversified portfolio. But if CAD 1,000 is the beginner's whole investment account, buying one railway creates avoidable concentration risk.

**A railway stock is not a substitute for a diversified portfolio.**

| Beginner situation | Better choice |
|---|---|
| CAD 1,000 is the whole portfolio | Use a diversified ETF first; put CN/CP on watchlist |
| Already owns broad ETFs | Add small CN or CP satellite if interested |
| Wants income and stability | CN is more natural |
| Wants North American trade growth | CPKC is more natural |
| Cannot choose | Split CN/CP or avoid the single-stock decision |

The beginner mistake is thinking "railways are safe" means "one railway stock is enough diversification." Railways are durable businesses, but the stocks are still equities.

## 10. Final Decision Memo

| Lens | CN | CPKC |
|---|---|---|
| Business quality | Excellent | Excellent |
| Moat | Very strong network moat | Very strong network moat plus unique Canada-U.S.-Mexico route |
| Current valuation | More reasonable | More expensive |
| Dividend | Better current yield and longer record | Lower yield, more reinvestment/growth emphasis |
| Growth story | Steady compounder | Stronger strategic upside if KCS synergies work |
| Beginner fit | Better single-stock candidate | Better growth candidate, but less forgiving valuation |

**Research conclusion:** For a Canadian beginner choosing only one railway stock today, CN is the cleaner pick because the valuation, dividend, and current financial profile are more forgiving. CPKC is a high-quality alternative but requires more confidence in growth and integration execution. If the investor is new and CAD 1,000 is the whole allocation, the better answer is to avoid single-stock concentration and start with a diversified ETF.

**Mirror test:** "I would buy CN or CPKC only if I understand that railways are cyclical, capital-intensive businesses; I am not relying on one stock for diversification; and I can hold through recessions, labour disputes, fuel-cost pressure, and valuation drawdowns."
