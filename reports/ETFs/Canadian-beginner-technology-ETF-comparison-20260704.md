**Research topic:** HXQ.TO, XQQ.TO, XIT.TO, and XGRO.TO as a risk comparison for a small CAD 1,000 allocation

## Executive View

For a beginner with only CAD 1,000, the main decision is not "which tech ETF will win next year." The better first question is: "How much concentrated technology risk do I want relative to a broad portfolio?"

My plain-language ranking by use case:

| Use case | More suitable fund | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| Simple U.S. megacap tech/growth exposure without currency hedging | **HXQ.TO** | Nasdaq-100 exposure, CAD-listed, no currency hedging, lower MER than XQQ. |
| U.S. megacap tech/growth exposure with CAD hedging | **XQQ.TO** | Similar Nasdaq-100 economic idea, but hedges USD/CAD currency moves. |
| A specific bet on Canadian technology stocks | **XIT.TO** | Canadian tech exposure, but very concentrated in a handful of names. |
| A beginner's core holding or risk benchmark | **XGRO.TO** | Globally diversified all-in-one portfolio targeting about 80% equity and 20% fixed income. |

For a beginner, the cleaner structure is usually to treat HXQ/XQQ/XIT as satellite holdings, not the whole portfolio. XGRO is included as the benchmark because it shows how different a diversified portfolio feels: more boring, less thematic, and less dependent on a small group of technology companies.

## Nasdaq Exposure vs Canadian Tech Exposure

HXQ and XQQ are Nasdaq-100 funds. They are mostly U.S. large-cap growth exposure. The holdings are global businesses, but the economic and currency exposure is heavily tied to U.S. equity markets and the U.S. dollar.

XIT is Canadian technology exposure. Its return depends heavily on a small group of Canadian tech and tech-adjacent companies. Shopify is e-commerce software, Celestica is electronics manufacturing and supply-chain exposure, Constellation Software is vertical-market software, CGI is IT services, and Descartes is logistics software.

The key difference:

| Question | HXQ / XQQ | XIT |
|---|---|---|
| Main country exposure | Mostly United States | Canada |
| Main style | Large-cap growth / Nasdaq | Concentrated Canadian tech |
| AI exposure | Indirect, through Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Meta, Amazon, AMD, etc. | Less direct AI megacap exposure; more Canadian software and hardware supply-chain exposure |
| Concentration | Broad across 100-plus securities, but megacap-heavy | Very high: top three around 73.76% |
| Currency issue | Important for HXQ; hedged in XQQ | Mostly CAD-listed Canadian-company exposure |

## Currency Hedging vs Unhedged Exposure

Currency hedging is an attempt to reduce the effect of exchange-rate moves. For a Canadian buying U.S. assets, the two moving parts are the U.S. stock return and USD/CAD.

HXQ is CAD-listed but not currency-hedged. If the U.S. dollar rises versus the Canadian dollar, that can help a Canadian holder. If the Canadian dollar rises versus the U.S. dollar, that can hurt.

XQQ is CAD-hedged. It attempts to reduce USD/CAD swings, so the return should be closer to the Nasdaq-100's equity return in CAD-hedged terms. Hedging is not free or perfect. It can help in some currency regimes and hurt in others.

XIT is mostly Canadian-listed technology exposure, so USD/CAD is less central than it is for a Nasdaq-100 ETF. Some holdings still earn revenue globally, so currency can still affect company results.

XGRO has a mix. It holds Canadian, U.S., international, and bond ETFs. Some exposures are naturally foreign-currency exposed; some bond exposures may be hedged through the underlying funds. For a beginner, the important point is that currency risk is diluted across more asset classes and regions.

## Bull Case

The bull case for HXQ and XQQ is that AI, cloud computing, semiconductors, digital advertising, e-commerce, and software keep compounding through the largest global technology platforms. Nvidia remains a key AI infrastructure holding, while Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Apple, AMD, and others provide multiple ways to participate in the AI/data economy.

The bull case for XIT is different. Canada has a smaller but investable technology market, and the current basket includes companies with global revenue exposure. Shopify, Celestica, Constellation Software, CGI, and Descartes are not interchangeable businesses, so XIT gives a domestic tech tilt that broad Canadian index ETFs may underweight.

The bull case for XGRO is that beginners often win by avoiding avoidable mistakes: owning a diversified portfolio, rebalancing automatically, paying low fees, and not making the entire account depend on one theme.

## Bear Case

The bear case for HXQ and XQQ is valuation. XQQ's portfolio P/E was 41.64 and P/B was 9.86 as of July 2, 2026. Those figures can be justified if earnings grow strongly, but they leave less room for disappointment. If AI spending slows, margins compress, antitrust pressure rises, or rates move higher, Nasdaq-style growth stocks can fall sharply.

The bear case for XIT is concentration. A beginner may think they are buying "Canadian tech," but the top three holdings were about 73.76%. If Shopify, Celestica, or Constellation Software disappoint, XIT can behave more like a small portfolio of big positions than a broad ETF.

The bear case for XGRO is opportunity cost. If technology keeps leading the market, XGRO will probably lag a pure tech ETF because it also owns bonds, Canadian equities, international equities, and emerging markets. That is the point of diversification, but it can feel frustrating during a tech bull market.

| Risk | What it means | Most relevant ETFs |

## Beginner Mistakes To Avoid

1. Do not confuse CAD-listed with Canadian exposure. HXQ and XQQ trade in Canada, but their economic exposure is mostly U.S. large-cap growth.
2. Do not assume an ETF is automatically diversified. XIT is an ETF, but its top holdings are highly concentrated.
3. Do not buy only because of AI headlines. Nasdaq ETFs already embed large AI expectations through Nvidia and other megacaps.
4. Do not ignore currency. HXQ and XQQ can both own similar stocks but produce different CAD returns because of hedging.
5. Do not treat a CAD 1,000 first investment as a prediction contest. Building habits, keeping fees low, and avoiding panic selling can matter more than choosing the hottest ETF.
6. Do not compare returns without comparing risk. XGRO is supposed to be less concentrated than the tech ETFs.

## Bottom Line

For a Canadian beginner, HXQ and XQQ are the cleanest ways in this group to get broad Nasdaq-100 growth exposure. HXQ is cheaper and unhedged; XQQ is hedged and slightly more expensive. XIT is a narrower Canadian technology bet and should be treated as more concentrated than the word "ETF" might suggest. XGRO is not the exciting answer, but it is the most useful risk benchmark: it shows what a broadly diversified beginner portfolio looks like before adding a tech satellite.

If the CAD 1,000 is the investor's first or only investment, a diversified all-in-one ETF such as XGRO may be the more robust starting point. If it is a small satellite beside a diversified core, HXQ or XQQ is easier to justify than making XIT a large position.
